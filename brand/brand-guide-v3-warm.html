Skin over Steel

The name still carries its heritage — training logs, earned trust, private rooms — but the surface finally matches the mission. v3 trades the dark forge floor for a warm, welcoming ground: a calm clinic, not a workshop. Steel is still underneath; the skin over it is the part people meet. A tool with a community inside it, that looks glad you came.

01

Why warm, and why now

Men put off these conversations because the places that host them feel clinical, transactional, or ashamed on their behalf. The steel engineering stays — the elevation system, the evidence grades, the instrument voice — but it now sits under a cream ground and a human, sentence-case display voice. Reducing the activation energy to ask for help is the whole product; the surface should do its part.

02

Marks on warm

Same three marks — the system was built to re-theme. On cream, STEEL goes ink, Skin stays copper (deepened to #B0462A), and the monogram becomes a saturated copper tile with a cream counter, so the favicon still pops at 16px instead of washing out.

A · Wordmark. STEEL is warm ink; Skin stays copper. The warm voice is the only accent on the page.

B · Monogram / app icon. A saturated copper tile with a cream "S", so the mark stays legible at favicon and home-screen sizes.

C · Signal mark. Ink dots, copper dashes. It still doubles as the app's loading state — the Morse rhythm pulses, and degrades to the static mark under reduced motion.

Established / Emerging / Anecdote — outline badges only

Contrast floor: body text ≥ 7:1 on E0–E2 (Ink on Cream Ground ≈ 13:1). Every accent ink — copper and the grade tones — clears WCAG AA ≥ 4.5:1 on the darkest surface it sits on (the E1 panel), verified by the axe a11y suite. Copper is for interaction and identity, never for paragraphs. Evidence colors never fill — outline badges only, so grades read as instrumentation, not mood. The medical-emergency red is a deeper true-crimson (#B81E12), held deliberately distinct from the warm terracotta accent so a safety signal never reads as a CTA.

06

Voice, unchanged — register, warmed

The voice rules stand: candid, evidence-led, unashamed, no hype, no shame, hard lines on dosing content. The warm surface tunes one register — the editorial and community voice leans a little further toward welcome, while the UI keeps its instrument calm.

Instrument voice (UI)

Terse, mono, factual. "Log saved. Week 9 of 12." · "3 sources attached." · "Export ready." The interface never cheers, congratulates with confetti, or guilt-trips a missed week. Respect is the tone — now on a surface that isn't trying to look tough.

Human voice (editorial & community)

Warm serif, first-person welcome, sentence-case headlines, direct about anatomy and feelings alike. The italic copper voice carries anything vulnerable. Members are members; asking for help is the smart move, said plainly and without a dark room around it.
